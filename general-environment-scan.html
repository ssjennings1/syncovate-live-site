Syncovate A Saturday Seed Working Guide

The General
Environment Scan

What's coming at you from outside, and what your team makes of it.

Before you start

This is not solo homework.
Do not fill this out alone at your desk.

The seven categories below are a way of looking at the world outside your walls: what's changing out there, and what it might mean for your company, your people, your customers, and everybody you depend on.

Getting the information down is useful. But the real value is in the conversation. When your team works through this together, out loud, they end up with the same picture in their heads. That's worth more than the list.

How to run it

Break your team into small groups.

Give each group one or two categories to own, then have them report out to the whole room.

Live in the room

This is how I usually run it, inside a strategy session. Groups get real time to work their categories, not five minutes. They talk it through, write down what they know and what they're not sure about, and then present to everybody. It moves, and the room feeds off each other.

Or send them out first

If you want harder information, give the groups a week before you meet. This is public information and most of it is free. Economic reports from your state and your region. What the experts in your area are saying, at the university, the chamber, the trade association. And when it applies to you, what the big publicly traded companies in or around your world are telling their investors, because they pay people a lot of money to see what's coming, and they publish it.

Use your library

Most people forget this one. Your public library will likely have access to business and market research databases you would otherwise pay for, and often you can get in from home with a library card. Many libraries also have a librarian who can help you dig, or can point you to other resources. Worth a phone call before you spend money on this.

Either way, the report-out is the part that matters. Each group takes their turn: here's what we looked at, here's what we found, here's what we think it might mean for us. Then the rest of the room asks questions and adds what they know.

Your job in the report-out is not to correct anybody or supply the right answer. Ask good questions. Let the room work.

Two rules for the room
Suspend judgment.

You're not deciding yet whether something is good news or bad news. The same fact can be a threat or an opportunity depending on the company. Just get it on the page.

Watch for weak signals.

Don't just collect established facts. Pay attention to the weird, unexpected, or seemingly minor shifts you've noticed lately that make you a little uncomfortable.

The Seven Categories

Scan the world outside your walls.

1Demographic
The people

Who lives and works around you, and how that's shifting. Population going up or down. People getting older or younger. Where they're moving to and from. Who has money to spend and who doesn't.

Thought starters
  • Is our county growing or shrinking, and what does the projection say ten years out?
  • What's happening to the age of our workforce, and who's coming up behind them?
  • Where is household income around here headed?
Notes
2Economic
The money

Where the economy is headed in the places you do business. Interest rates, material costs, wages, whether people are spending or sitting on their hands.

Thought starters
  • What are the regional forecasts saying about our part of the country?
  • What's the outlook on interest rates, and what does that do to the people who buy from us?
  • What's happening to wages around here, and can we still compete for people?
Notes
3Political / Legal
The rules

The laws, regulations, codes, and permits you operate under, plus the people and groups pushing to change them. Local, state, and federal.

Thought starters
  • What rule or code changes are working their way toward us?
  • What's on the ballot or in front of the legislature that touches our work?
  • Who's making decisions that affect us, and are we paying any attention to them?
Notes
4Socio-cultural
How people live

What people believe, expect, and value, and how that's changing. How they want to work. How they want to buy. What they'll put up with and what they won't.

Thought starters
  • What do people expect from a company like ours that they didn't expect five years ago?
  • What are younger workers looking for, and where are they finding it?
  • What's changing about how people around here want to buy?
Notes
5Technological
The tools

New tools, new methods, new equipment, and the people creating them. Some of it makes you faster. Some of it makes what you do less valuable.

Thought starters
  • What part of our work will a machine or software do cheaper within a few years?
  • Where are competitors using tools that let them run leaner or move faster than us?
  • What have we bought in the last two years that nobody uses?
Notes
6Global
The wide world

Markets, supply chains, and events beyond your borders. You may not sell overseas, but your materials, your equipment, and your prices travel.

Thought starters
  • What's happening with tariffs, trade rules, or currency that touches what we buy?
  • What global disruption would hurt us most, and how would we find out it was coming?
  • Is there demand for what we do somewhere we've never looked?
Notes
7Physical
The ground under you

The actual physical world you work in. Weather, land, water, energy, buildings, roads. What's changing about the conditions you operate in.

Thought starters
  • What weather or seasonal patterns are different than they used to be?
  • What's happening to the cost or availability of energy, water, or land here?
  • What's planned for the roads, utilities, and land around us?
Notes
When you're done

Look at what your team put on the page. Some of it will turn into an opportunity, something out there you could use. Some of it will turn into a threat, something that could get in your way. You'll sort that out later.

For now, notice two things. You know more about the outside world than you did an hour ago. And your team just practiced thinking through something messy together and landing in the same place.

That second one is the skill. This was the practice.

After the report-outs

The synthesis

Work through these together after the report-outs.

The top three surprises

What did we hear today that challenged what we thought we knew?

The blind spot

Which of the seven categories do we know the least about as a team?

The watchlist

What two things out there do we need to keep an eye on before our next strategy session?
