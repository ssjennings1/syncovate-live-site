What I'm Good At

What I've been told, more than once.

You don't talk down to me.

Correct. If you built it by doing, you know more than you're giving yourself credit for. I'm here to sharpen that, not replace it.

You don't take sides.

Owner, partner, family, crew — I'm not there to win anyone's argument. That's why people tell me the things they've been holding back.

You don't get distracted by feelings.

True, but not because I avoid them. I'm a psychologist, and I spent my early career as a therapist, which means I've sat with things heavier than most people can imagine. I can feel what you're going through and keep my head in the game. So when the succession talk gets hot, or a partner or family member finally says what they've been holding back for ten years, I don't try to fix it or smooth it over. That's real information. Feelings show up in the business — in the decisions you make and the ones you keep putting off. Avoid them and they run the place. Sit in them and nothing moves. We put them on the table, figure them out, and turn it into our advantage.

You don't stop at questions.

Questions are the easy part. I come back with ideas, resources, and a plan — and I'm there when you have to run it.

You catch it early.

Most of what I do is help you see the problem while it's still small enough to fix.

How I Got Here

The problem you bring me
is almost never the real problem.

I learned that first as a therapist, working with families in conflict. Then in twenty years and a couple hundred rooms with owners — shop floors, job sites, family companies where the org chart and the dinner table are the same table. The pattern never changes: the thing that's stuck was built by good people, on purpose, for good reasons. Which is exactly why you can't see it from inside.

Somewhere in there I got a doctorate in business psychology. It put research behind the pattern. The rooms are still where I learned it.
