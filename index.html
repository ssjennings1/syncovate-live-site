You used to know everything. Now you decide everything.

Those aren't the same thing, and somewhere along the way one quietly replaced the other. When the company was small, you saw all of it — every customer, every job, every fire. Now it's bigger than one person can see, so your people see it for you, and they hand you the parts they think you can handle.

Nobody's lying to you. They think they're doing their job. But there's an old story about an emperor and a set of clothes, and it doesn't end with the emperor figuring it out on his own.

Here's what it looks like from where you sit:

Meetings that produce motion but not movement.
A leadership team that agrees in the room and does their own thing once they leave.
You keep hearing "we have a communication problem."
Two of your best people keep coming to you about the other — and it's yours to fix.
Someone in a role they can't grow into, and everyone knows it.
Everyone's waiting on you. You feel like you're waiting on them.

Nobody's doing anything wrong. It's a pattern — and it's impossible to spot from the middle of it.

We find the gap between where you think you are and where you actually are. Then we figure out the next best step — and take it.

"In twenty years of this work, I've never walked into a leadership system where the blind spot was created by bad intentions. It's almost always good intentions — and the assumptions that grew up quietly underneath them."
