How I work with owners and their teams

When people ask why they should work with me, I come back to one thing: being heard.

"It is astonishing how elements which seem insoluble become soluble when someone listens."— Carl Rogers, On Becoming a Person

At heart I'm a listener. That's where my training started, as a therapist, and it's still the first thing I do in any room. But being heard isn't enough. To move forward and make a change takes tools, practice, and somebody who will stick with you all the way to action.

I've done this work for twenty years with founders, CEOs, and leadership teams in the trades, manufacturing, family business, and professional services. The conversations are different every single time (I never get bored), but the pattern underneath them rarely is. That's the part I'm good at seeing.

Work with me directly.

Some leaders want a standing partner, somebody in their corner between the big moments. You text me when something comes up (a conversation that went sideways, a hire you're on the fence about, a family issue that is bleeding into the business), and we either work it in real time or hold it for our next session. We work on the thing that matters right now.

Bring me in for your team.

When a leadership team stops being honest with each other, it costs you in ways that never show up on a report: decisions that stall, good people who go quiet, the same meeting over and over again. Facilitation is my favorite part of this work, and it's a simple, straightforward place to start. You provide the room and the snacks. I bring a session I've run many, many times, your people leave with something they can use on Monday, and you get a real feel for how I work before anybody commits to anything bigger.
