### Hard to see from the inside

## Hard to see from the inside.

The way decisions get made, the way your people talk to each other, the way work moves through the shop. All of it made sense when you built it. Then the company grew, and the things that held it together started getting in the way.

Here's what that looks like:

- Good people who aren't doing their best work.
- Meetings that end with a decision, and then nothing happens.
- A leadership team that agrees in the room and goes its own way after.
- Problems between your people that keep landing on your desk.
- Someone you love in a role they can't grow into, and everyone can feel it.

In twenty years, I've never walked into a company where the problem started with bad intentions. It almost always starts with good ones, and the habits that grew up quietly around them.

# How I work with owners and their teams.

When people ask why they should work with me, I come back to one thing: being heard.

> "It is astonishing how elements that seem insoluble become soluble when someone listens." — Carl Rogers

At heart, I'm a listener. That's where my training started. It's not where I stopped. Being heard opens the door. What you do next is what changes things. That takes tools, and it takes someone who will help you get all the way to action.

For twenty years I've done this work with founders, CEOs, and leadership teams. Trades, manufacturing, family business, professional services. The conversations are different every time. The pattern underneath them rarely is.

### Who finds their way here

## Who finds their way here.

Usually it's the owner who calls me. Sometimes it's for themselves, and sometimes it's for someone they're counting on, who then comes here to check me out. Either way, if you see yourself below, I'm probably the right fit.

**The Founder / CEO**
### You used to have the answers. Now you're not so sure.
Whether you started the business from scratch or built it from a solid foundation, you got it here with your own know-how and hard work. Now a lot of people build their lives around it. You used to know every job and every number. Now you're managing managers, and a wrong call that once cost you a bad week lands on thirty families. **The Middle Manager**
### Great at the work. New to leading it at this level.
You moved up because you're the best at what you do. Foreman to operations manager. Field to the office. Line lead to section lead. One store to several. The work you were great at didn't change. What changed is that now you lead the people doing it, and you're figuring that out on the job, while the job keeps coming. We build your toolbox together: how to run a meeting, give feedback, set expectations, and handle the conversations you've been avoiding. Practical stuff you can use the same week.
"I know my stuff. I just don't always know how to get people to follow me."

### You're not just stepping into what your family built. You're stepping into their expectations.
The business comes with history, and so does your seat at the table. The people before you built it one way. You see where it needs to go next. I've spent twenty years with families where the family and the business are the same thing, and I don't take sides. We sort out what's worth keeping, what's yours to change, and how to talk about both.
"I want to honor what was built and still make it mine."

### It doesn't matter what we call it

## It doesn't matter what we call it.

Coaching, advising, consulting, facilitation. The label matters a lot less than what you walk out with. Here's what that looks like, whether it's a room of twelve or one-on-one.

**You feel heard. Really heard.** Not managed. Not judged. I'm curious about what you're seeing, what you're carrying, and the thing you almost said but didn't. I ask questions until the picture gets clear.

**You see something you couldn't see before.** Not because I told you what it was. Because together we connected the dots: things you've said, things your people have said, things that happened years ago and are still showing up today. Then you see it, and it's obvious.

**You leave with something every time.** Insight without action is just a nice conversation. Once the smoke clears, we figure out the next real move together. No forty-page deck. No jargon. Just what's next.

**You did it. Not me.** That's the part I love most and get the least credit for. You had this in you the whole time. You needed a partner and the space to think. I bring both.

### Origin

## The problem people bring you is almost never the real problem.

I learned that early, working with families in the hardest situations. Divorce, custody, the stuff where somebody's kids are on the line. The real problem was never the thing they walked in with. It was the unspoken rules around them. What everybody knew behind closed doors and nobody said in the hallway.

I carried that into business at the Edward Lowe Foundation, running peer retreats for entrepreneurs and CEOs. Their whole mission was to champion the entrepreneurial spirit, and I'm still living it more than a decade after I left. These companies matter. The ones growing in your town, hiring locally, sponsoring the Little League, putting money back in. They hold communities together, especially now. That's who I've built my work around.

Here's what all those rooms taught me: when you build something, you're in it. The business and the person aren't two separate things. Your money, your identity, your relationships, how you sleep, all of it is tied up in the thing you made. That's true for the contractor and the manufacturer and the professional-services owner, and it gets even more tangled in a family business, where the succession talk is also a family talk and the dinner table and the org chart are the same table. You have to see the whole picture to be any real use. That's the work I love most.

### Why a Business Psychologist?

## Why a Business Psychologist?

My doctorate in business psychology put real, evidence-based structure around what a decade of closed doors had already taught me. Leaders with the best of intentions, working incredibly hard, and their teams fracturing anyway. Everyone assumed it was a people problem. Fix the difficult personality, hire better talent.

Look closer and it's rarely a people problem. It's how the company is built, and you can't see that from inside it. Not because anything's wrong with you. Because you're inside it. My job is to be the person who can, and to stay in the room until something moves.

### Direct. Not one-sided. In it with you.

## Direct. Not one-sided. In it with you.

If you want someone to nod along and tell you the plan looks great, I'm not the right fit.

**I listen first.** Long enough that you'll probably say the thing you weren't planning to say. Then I say my piece, plainly.

**I don't take sides.** Not the owner's, not the team's, not the family's. That's usually why people open up in the first place.

**I won't talk down to you.** If you built your leadership by doing it instead of studying it, you know more than you give yourself credit for. I'm not here to replace what you've got. I'm here to help you see it clearly and use it better.

**I don't disappear.** Insight is the easy part. The hard part is next Tuesday, when you have to do something with it. Text me when it comes up. We work it then, not three weeks later.

The work takes whatever shape the situation calls for. One session to get a stuck decision unstuck. A year of monthly calls that become the one place you can think out loud without consequences. Me in a room with your team when they've stopped being honest with each other. And when I stop being useful, I'll say so.
